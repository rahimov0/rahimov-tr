What do the buying and selling prices mean?

The buying price is what a bank or exchange office pays you for your currency — the amount you get when you sell your dollars. The selling price is what they charge when they sell currency to you. The selling price is always higher than the buying price. The gap between them is called the spread, and it is how they make money.

So if you buy 100 dollars and sell them straight back, you lose a few lira even if the rate has not moved at all. The spread is narrow while the market is open and wide in the evening and at weekends. That is why prices in banking apps look worse outside working hours.

Why is the rate here different from my bank's?

The prices in the table are average free-market buying and selling prices. Every bank and exchange office adds its own spread on top. In Turkey, buying foreign currency also incurs a foreign exchange tax (BSMV) on the amount, which raises the total you pay. So the price at your bank will be somewhat different from the one here. For large amounts it pays to compare a few providers.

How does the converter work?

The converter has three options. I'm buying currency uses the selling price, I'm selling currency uses the buying price and Average uses the midpoint. When converting between two foreign currencies (e.g. euro → dollar) it calculates a cross rate via their lira values. The result is approximate; what you actually get depends on the price of the institution you deal with.

What is the central bank rate?

The Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT) publishes indicative exchange rates every working day in the afternoon. These rates are used in official transactions, tax calculations, invoices and contracts. The live free-market price can drift away from the CBRT rate during the day. The chart on this page is based on daily reference rates of this kind, while the table shows market prices.

Frequently asked questions

How often are the rates updated?

The table refreshes every two minutes while the page is open. The source's last update time is shown at the top of the page. Markets are closed at weekends, so prices barely move then.

What is the daily change based on?

The percentage change shows today's difference from the previous day's closing price. A green up arrow means a rise, a red down arrow a fall.

Can I trade at these prices?

No. This page is for information only; you cannot trade here. Use the current price of the bank or exchange office you deal with.

What is the data source?

Buying and selling prices come from Truncgil Finans; chart data comes from central bank reference rates compiled by Frankfurter and from the open currency archive on jsDelivr.
